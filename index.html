The deadlines are real, and one has already passed

Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 covers every product with digital elements sold in the EU, including products from manufacturers outside the EU. Microcontroller firmware is in scope.

In force
11 Sep 2026

Actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents must be reported to ENISA: early warning within 24 h, notification within 72 h, final report within 14 days.

Most SBOM tools can't see inside your firmware

An SBOM (software bill of materials) is an ingredients list for your firmware: every third-party software component (RTOS, TCP/IP stack, encryption library, vendor drivers) and its exact version. With it you can tell within hours whether a newly published vulnerability affects your products. The CRA requires one.

Syft, Trivy and similar tools look for package-manager lockfiles. A typical STM32 or NXP project has none: FreeRTOS, Mbed TLS, lwIP and the HAL are vendored straight into the tree. The result is an empty SBOM, or one that lists code the linker threw away.

FAQ

We're a US company. Does the CRA apply to us?

Yes, if you sell products with digital elements on the EU market. The CRA applies to manufacturers wherever they are based, and importers and distributors have obligations too.

Our product is a bare-metal microcontroller with no OS. Is it in scope?

Generally, yes. The CRA covers hardware and software products with a data connection, whether direct or indirect, and there is no exemption for firmware without an operating system. A few sectors with their own rules (for example, medical devices and motor vehicles) are excluded.

Do we need an SBOM before December 2027?

The formal SBOM requirement applies from 11 Dec 2027. But to meet the reporting deadlines already in force, you need to know within hours whether a newly exploited CVE affects your products. In practice, that takes an SBOM.
